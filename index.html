We started with empathy, not solutions 👂

Before writing a line of code we interviewed technicians, stall owners and shoppers in Computer Village. Three voices kept coming back:

"I bought a 'original' screwdriver set. The tips rounded off in one week. ₦9,500 gone."

— Phone repair tech, Ola-Ayeni street, Ikeja

"The same hot-air station is ₦40,000 in one shop and ₦55,000 two streets away. Who is cheating who?"

— Laptop repair tech & new shop owner

"When my tool fails mid-repair, the customer blames me, not the tool. I lose the customer."

— Motherboard-level technician, 7 yrs experience

Root problem, not symptoms 🌱

Symptom: technicians buy bad tools.
Root problem: the market has no trusted way to verify authenticity or compare prices before money changes hands. Trust is personal ("my guy"), not verifiable — so fakes thrive and newcomers get exploited.

Our testable problem statement

P1: Technicians cannot confirm a tool is genuine before purchase.
P2: Tool prices for identical items vary up to ~35% within the same market with no reference point.
P3: Restocking requires physically walking stalls — 2–4 hours lost per restock trip.

Why we can win 🏆

Impact: solves a documented, local, livelihood-threatening problem.
Feasibility: zero-infrastructure MVP — works on any phone browser, offline catalog, WhatsApp rails the market already uses.
UX: mobile-first, low-literacy friendly, big tap targets, naira pricing.
Technical craft: deterministic batch-code verification, local-first data, clean component architecture.
